# bzz v0.12.1 — Quota-aware relay admission and subscription recovery

**Status:** Approved and implemented locally 2026-08-28; release gates pending

**Prerequisite:** publish and verify v0.12.0 before tagging v0.12.1.

**Protocol baseline:** unchanged `block/buzz`
`9f55bf67456be10ff7c8238bf0d9e12e582848f6`

## Release thesis

v0.12.1 is a client-correctness patch for relay admission control. bzz currently
opens every joined-channel subscription immediately after authentication. With
66 joined channels, the four baseline subscriptions and selected-channel typing
produce about 71 immediate NIP-01 `REQ` frames. The deployed Buzz relay admits
50 `REQ`/`EVENT`/`COUNT` frames per principal in its five-second WebSocket
window, so a healthy authenticated startup can receive `CLOSED rate-limited:`
responses and an adjacent publication can be rejected.

The durable fix is client-side pacing and recovery. bzz will place billable
WebSocket operations behind one bounded admission scheduler, prioritize
publications and the visible conversation, retain rate-limited subscriptions in
the desired set, and retry them after the relay hint with bounded jitter. A
quota response must not become `AccessDenied`, permanently disable typing, tear
down a healthy socket, or leave a sticky raw relay notice.

This release does not change Buzz relay, Traefik, Kubernetes, Buzz Desktop, the
server quota, or the pinned compatibility baseline. Correlated relay rejection
and Buzz Desktop startup pacing remain separate upstream/infrastructure work.

## Evidence and current failure mode

Read-only production correlation established:

- WebSocket connection and NIP-42 authentication succeeded;
- Buzz and Traefik remained healthy;
- the relay rejected WebSocket frames for `reason="quota"`, not HTTP 429,
  connection pressure, Valkey failure, pod saturation, or ingress throttling;
- bzz opened about 71 frames against a 50-frame/five-second budget;
- 22 quota rejections occurred in the observed startup burst;
- the burst stopped, but bzz retained the last notice in the footer; and
- the same principal budget is shared across concurrent sockets, so Buzz
  Desktop and bzz can consume each other's headroom.

The current source explains the behavior:

1. `App::start_sync` enqueues four baseline subscriptions and up to 900 channel
   subscriptions without pacing.
2. `realtime::supervisor` immediately replays every desired subscription after
   reconnect.
3. `realtime::session` writes queued commands directly to the socket and uses a
   command-biased select loop.
4. every relay-originated `CLOSED` currently removes the subscription from the
   supervisor's desired set.
5. app-level channel `CLOSED` handling maps the failure to `AccessDenied`; the
   typing subscription is quarantined as unavailable.
6. a raw `NOTICE` becomes a sticky status error. An old relay's uncorrelated
   rate-limit `NOTICE` can settle only the special case with exactly one pending
   publication.

## Product decisions

### v0.12.1, not v0.13.0

This is a correctness repair to v0.12.0. It introduces no protocol, database
schema, trust boundary, configuration surface, or new user capability.

### Proactive pacing belongs in bzz

A relay quota increase can reduce symptoms but cannot make clients with hundreds
of channels safe. bzz must remain well-behaved against the current relay and
other bounded relays without requiring an infrastructure override.

Use a conservative fixed client budget of eight billable WebSocket frames per
second. This leaves headroom below the current ten-per-second human allowance
and keeps the maximum steady five-second contribution at 40 frames.

The value is an internal compatibility constant, not a user configuration key.
No runtime quota probing or private relay API is added.

### Publications outrank background subscriptions

The scheduler priority is:

1. an already authorized and durably staged interactive publication;
2. selected-channel and selected-channel typing subscriptions;
3. baseline membership, personal, global-stream, and read-state subscriptions;
4. maintenance publications such as read-state/presence refreshes; and
5. background joined-channel subscriptions.

Add an internal `PublishPriority` at call sites rather than inferring user
intent from an event kind. Conversation sends, reactions, and explicit user
commands are interactive; periodic/coalesced maintenance is not. Existing
outbox recovery keeps its acknowledgement-aware ordering and uses a bounded
recovery priority below a new interactive send.

`AUTH`, `CLOSE`, WebSocket close, ping, and pong are control frames and remain
immediate. bzz does not emit `COUNT` today. A pending background subscription
or maintenance publication must never delay a human publication behind
hundreds of channels.

### Rate-limited subscriptions remain desired

A `CLOSED rate-limited:` response means the individual wire subscription ended;
it does not mean access was denied or the authenticated connection failed. Keep
the latest desired filter, arm the community-scoped gate, and reopen it after
the retry hint plus jitter.

Terminal authorization or malformed-filter closures remain terminal and are not
retried. Generic transient closures use exponential backoff. The policy must be
pure, table-driven, and shared by channel, baseline, and typing subscriptions.

### Do not blindly republish rejected events

Pacing may delay an EVENT before its first wire send, but it must not create a
new publication intent. Once the relay explicitly returns
`OK(event_id, false, ...)`, v0.12.1 does not silently publish that event again.
The acknowledgement-aware outbox records the rejection, restores the exact
draft generation, and leaves explicit retry to the user and existing outbox
workflow.

For compatibility with an older relay that emits an uncorrelated rate-limit
`NOTICE`, permit at most one in-flight EVENT. Treat that event as delivery
uncertain, preserve the draft, and never infer acceptance.

### Online remains online

A quota gate is degraded operation on an authenticated socket, not an offline,
terminal, or access-denied connection. Present a bounded transient status such
as:

```text
relay busy · retrying in 1s
```

The notice clears when the gate expires unless another actionable status has
superseded it. A countdown may redraw at most once per second and must preserve
the one-row responsive v0.12 status bar.

## M0 — Semantic, privacy, and compatibility boundaries

1. Keep the pinned Buzz baseline unchanged.
2. Add no relay, Traefik, Kubernetes, ClickHouse, or Buzz Desktop change.
3. Add no schema migration, configuration key, dependency, HTTP polling path,
   or protocol kind.
4. Preserve locked mode, identity isolation, Inbox/read state, drafts, outbox,
   and exact accepted-delivery draft deletion.
5. Keep explicit human send as the only local human publication boundary.
6. Never log or diagnose relay reason text, filters, subscription IDs, event
   IDs, relay URLs, community/channel IDs, pubkeys, names, tags, or content as
   part of the new admission evidence.
7. Clamp untrusted retry hints and bound every queue, timer, and retry counter.

**Exit:** the patch changes only transport scheduling, retry classification,
and truthful presentation of temporary backpressure.

## M1 — Bounded outbound admission scheduler

Add `src/realtime/admission.rs` with a pure, clock-injectable scheduler model.

Requirements:

1. Admit no more than one billable wire frame every 125 ms.
2. Keep at most one in-flight publication and separate bounded interactive,
   recovery, and maintenance publication queues.
3. Store subscriptions by stable ID and latest filter revision rather than
   copying repeated retry work into an unbounded queue.
4. Coalesce duplicate `Subscribe(id, filters)` commands; same-ID replacement
   sends only the newest filter.
5. A `Close(id)` cancels an unsent subscription immediately or sends at most one
   wire `CLOSE` for an open subscription.
6. Reconnect reconstructs work from the desired registry and reuses the same
   pacing and priority policy.
7. Reorder startup so the selected conversation and typing request are admitted
   before background channels.
8. Commands received while a gate is active update desired state without
   bypassing the gate.
9. Remove command-side starvation of inbound frames so a relay hint can stop
   later sends before the command backlog drains.
10. Queue waiting is bounded. If a publication cannot reach the wire within the
    local admission deadline, fail locally as retryable without claiming
    delivery uncertainty because no EVENT was sent.

The scheduler must remain deterministic under paused Tokio time; random jitter
is injected behind a testable interface.

**Exit:** a 900-channel desired set remains memory bounded, selected scope
recovers first, and startup/reconnect cannot produce a client-originated quota
burst under an otherwise idle principal.

## M2 — CLOSED and rate-limit recovery policy

Introduce a typed closure policy independent from diagnostic classification:

- `RateLimited { retry_after }`;
- `Retryable`;
- `TerminalAccess`;
- `TerminalProtocol`.

Rules:

1. Recognize only bounded canonical prefixes and immediately discard source
   text after classification.
2. Parse `retry in Ns` case-insensitively, use ten seconds when absent/zero, and
   cap the result at 300 seconds.
3. Overlapping hints extend to the latest expiry and never shorten an active
   gate.
4. Add small bounded jitter after the advertised deadline to avoid synchronized
   retries.
5. Rate-limited closure keeps the latest desired subscription and schedules one
   reopen.
6. A delivered event or EOSE resets that subscription's transient retry count.
7. Generic retryable closures back off exponentially from one to 30 seconds.
8. `restricted:`, `blocked:`, malformed/invalid filters, unsupported requests,
   and too-many-subscriptions remain terminal.
9. A locally initiated `CLOSE` acknowledgement remains consumed exactly once and
   cannot enter retry classification or recreate the old CLOSE/CLOSED loop.
10. A same-ID filter replacement during backoff retries only the newest desired
    revision.
11. Channel and typing rate-limit closures do not set `AccessDenied`, remove
    membership authority, trigger directory refresh, or permanently quarantine
    typing.
12. Genuine terminal typing closure retains the existing bounded unavailable
    behavior and content-free diagnostic.

**Exit:** temporary quota pressure self-recovers without a reconnect, hot loop,
subscription black hole, or stale-filter resurrection.

## M3 — Publication and status behavior

1. Route every supervisor EVENT through the same admission gate before it reaches
   the session actor.
2. Start the existing 25-second relay acknowledgement timeout only after the
   EVENT is written, not while it waits locally behind the gate.
3. Keep only one EVENT awaiting acknowledgement so an old relay's uncorrelated
   rate-limit NOTICE remains conservatively attributable.
4. `OK(event_id, false, "rate-limited:...")` completes immediately as a
   rejected acknowledgement, arms the gate, and preserves the exact draft.
5. An uncorrelated rate-limit NOTICE with the single in-flight event marks it
   uncertain; no automatic duplicate publication is allowed.
6. A rate-limit NOTICE with no pending event still arms the gate and schedules
   subscription recovery.
7. Replace raw relay quota text in the footer with fixed local wording and a
   bounded integer countdown.
8. Clear the notice automatically at gate expiry; do not retain the last quota
   error indefinitely.
9. Keep connection state online while authenticated. Genuine transport loss,
   terminal auth, and access denial retain existing semantics.
10. Outbox reconciliation remains sequential and acknowledgement-aware; no draft
    is deleted before exact acceptance or authoritative relay echo.

**Exit:** quota pressure delays safe first sends, rejects correlated failures
promptly, and never converts transport ambiguity into duplicate publication.

## M4 — Private observability

Add local-only, content-free admission evidence:

- `session.rate_limit_activated` with a fixed source enum
  (`closed`, `publish_ack`, or `notice`) and a bounded retry-duration bucket;
- `session.rate_limit_cleared`; and
- aggregate activation/recovery counts in `bzz status --json` and the human
  report.

Requirements:

1. Do not retain exact retry text or raw relay payloads.
2. Do not retain queue entries, counts by channel, filters, identifiers,
   identities, or URLs.
3. Exclude the new events from OTel pending a separate telemetry review.
4. Existing journals load with zero counts.
5. Support-report clearing remains unchanged.
6. Hostile reason strings and identity/path sentinels cannot enter the journal,
   status report, UI notice, test snapshot, or tracing output.

**Exit:** an owner can distinguish a quota gate from offline/access-denied state
without exposing communication metadata.

## M5 — Deterministic and integration validation

### Scheduler tests

- 71 immediate desired subscriptions produce no more than eight billable frames
  in any one-second interval and no more than 40 in five seconds;
- interactive publication preempts recovery/maintenance publications and queued
  background subscriptions;
- selected channel and typing precede maintenance and background channels;
- duplicate and same-ID replacement commands coalesce to the newest filter;
- pending close sends no REQ and an open close sends one CLOSE;
- reconnect uses the same paced ordering;
- 900 subscriptions remain bounded and eventually make progress;
- ping/pong, AUTH, shutdown, and CLOSE are not delayed by the data-frame budget;
- inbound CLOSED/NOTICE processing cannot starve behind commands; and
- local publication queue timeout occurs before wire send and is not marked
  delivery-uncertain.

### Recovery tests

- canonical one-second and multi-second hints;
- absent, zero, malformed, huge, mixed-case, and hostile hints;
- overlapping hints never shorten the gate;
- rate-limited CLOSED retries once after deadline plus jitter;
- repeated closures back off without a hot loop;
- EOSE/event resets retry state;
- terminal access/protocol closure never retries;
- locally acknowledged CLOSE never retries or emits a failure;
- replacement during backoff sends only the latest filter;
- typing recovers without a permanent unavailable message; and
- terminal typing closure remains quarantined.

### Publication tests

- queued publication is written before background REQs when admitted;
- acknowledgement timeout begins only after wire send;
- correlated `OK false rate-limited` resolves immediately and arms the gate;
- old-relay NOTICE with one pending EVENT becomes uncertain;
- no second EVENT is sent automatically after rejection or uncertainty;
- exact accepted ACK or authoritative echo remains the only draft deletion;
- queued cancellation/lock/shutdown sends no late event; and
- read-state, presence, and startup outbox recovery cannot starve new human
  conversation work.

### Fake and pinned relay acceptance

Extend the fake relay to record frame timestamps and simulate:

- a 50-frame/five-second fixed quota;
- REQ rejection through `CLOSED`;
- correlated EVENT rejection through `OK false`;
- legacy uncorrelated EVENT rejection through `NOTICE`; and
- close acknowledgements while retries are pending.

Pinned Buzz integration must prove:

- compatibility remains at `9f55bf67456be10ff7c8238bf0d9e12e582848f6`;
- an authenticated socket stays online through quota pressure;
- selected scope becomes live before background completion;
- every desired non-terminal subscription eventually reaches EOSE/live state;
- rate limiting does not recreate CLOSE/CLOSED feedback;
- publication/draft semantics remain acknowledgement-aware; and
- no raw reason or identifier enters new diagnostics.

### Visual acceptance

Use generated offline fixtures to review wide and narrow one-row status bars for:

- no quota gate;
- active one-second retry;
- long retry bucket;
- simultaneous verified-agent typing; and
- expiry returning the flexible status area without layout residue.

No production screenshot or private user data may enter fixtures or artifacts.

## Explicit non-goals

v0.12.1 does not:

- modify or deploy Buzz relay, Traefik, Kubernetes, Valkey, ClickHouse, or
  Cloudflare;
- raise `BUZZ_RATE_LIMIT_HUMAN_WS_EVENTS_PER_SEC`;
- merge or depend on upstream patch `0f029a7`;
- modify Buzz Desktop or claim to prevent its startup burst;
- aggregate channel filters or change relay authorization semantics;
- add automatic publication after an explicit rejection;
- alter event signing, content, tags, message kinds, structured mentions, or
  the human send boundary;
- add a database migration or persist scheduler/gate state;
- add NIP-AO, observer controls, local agents, runtime hosting, ACP, model/tool
  processes, or agent keys;
- publish typing or other presence activity; or
- change verified-agent authority, thread summaries, Inbox, search, unread,
  read-state semantics, clipboard, attachments, media, or theme groups.

## Definition of done

v0.12.1 is complete only when:

1. bzz proactively stays within its eight-frame-per-second WebSocket admission
   budget;
2. publications and selected conversation recovery outrank background channels;
3. rate-limited subscriptions remain desired and recover after bounded hinted
   backoff;
4. terminal closures remain fail-closed and never hot-loop;
5. a quota response never falsely becomes offline or access denied;
6. publication rejection/uncertainty cannot delete a draft or produce an
   automatic duplicate EVENT;
7. the fixed rate-limit notice is transient, one-row, and clears at gate expiry;
8. new diagnostics are local-only, bounded, content-free, and identifier-free;
9. the pinned relay, complete local suite, cross-platform CI, privacy checks,
   and production acceptance pass; and
10. no infrastructure, Buzz Desktop, private screenshot, credential, or
    unrelated working-tree change enters the release commit.
